# Writing and voice

Trail speaks like a careful colleague: short sentences, plain words, numbers with their context.

## Plain words

| Write | Not |
|---|---|
| Every visit counted | Unsampled |
| Bot visits left out | Bot hits excluded |
| Came directly | Direct / none |
| Points | pts, pp |
| % of total | Share |
| Waiting for the first event | Pending ingestion |
| Not enough data yet | Insufficient sample |
| on one day / over 9 days | in 1 day(s) |

Sentence case everywhere: buttons, headings, menu items, tabs. No exclamation marks in product. No "simply", "just", "easily". No emoji.

## Labelled comparisons

A number that changes always says what it changed from. "+86% vs Wed, Nov 11 (41 vs 22)". A KPI tile shows "vs 414". A rate difference is "+1.2 points". Small bases show the difference, not a percentage. See [dataviz.md](dataviz.md).

## Helper sentences

- Every empty state says what will appear and the one action that makes it appear: "Events show up here once your site sends one. Add the snippet, then open your site."
- Every metric with a definition gets a dotted underline that opens the definition on hover, focus or tap ("Bounce rate: visits with one page and no event. Single-page apps count route changes as pages.").
- Every error says what happened, whether data is safe, and what to do: "Could not load this range. Your data is safe. Try again."
- Every destructive confirmation names the thing: "Delete the goal Signup? Its history stays in your events."

## Numbers and units

Thin, tabular, no unit soup. "1,204 visitors", "4.2%", "1m 6s", "$1,240". En dash for ranges (Sep 22 – Oct 21). Times in the site's timezone, stated once ("India time").

## Labels

Buttons are verbs ("Add website", "Copy as API call"). Navigation is nouns named for content ("Acquisition", not "Insights"). Toasts are past tense and short ("Copied", "View saved"). Kbd hints use the platform glyph (⌘ on Mac, Ctrl elsewhere).

## Marketing

Same voice, more room. Claims are things the product does, with real numbers ("1.68 ms median ingest"), never superlatives. Never name competitors.
